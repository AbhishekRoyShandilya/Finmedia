# Finmedia — Strategy Research: AI-Powered Indian Market Intelligence Media

*Research date: 26 September 2026. This is business research, not legal advice. Have a SEBI-practice lawyer review the "Legal architecture" section before launch.*

---

## 0. TL;DR

1. **The idea is validated by this week's news.** IRDAI published its consultation paper *"Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution"* on **23 Sep 2026**. On 24 Sep, PB Fintech fell **up to 36%** to a 52-week low of ₹1,210, then fell another ~5% on 25 Sep. Management itself estimated a **60–70% cut to health-business NPV**. You had a whole evening to analyze it before the market opened. That evening is the product.
2. **A fully photorealistic *fictional* AI "expert" face is the single riskiest choice you could make.** On **16 Jul 2026** YouTube clarified that **AI personas giving advice on finance, health or legal topics cannot be monetized** (its own example: *"an AI podcast host giving investment advice"*). Enough of it can remove a whole channel from the Partner Program.
3. **"Realistic enough that people think it's real" isn't available anyway.** India's **IT Amendment Rules 2026 (in force since 20 Feb 2026)** make platforms show a **continuous, clearly visible label** on realistic synthetic video. You must declare it when you upload.
4. **What to do instead:** put a **real, named, credentialed human** at the center of the brand. Use a **disclosed digital twin of that person** (a licensed copy of your own face and voice) to scale routine formats. Use a **stylized non-human mascot** for Reels. Presenting the anchor as a real, accountable person is what keeps it monetizable, and in finance it is also more trusted.
5. **Your signature format (the Policybazaar-style call) legally counts as a "research report."** A company-specific view that gives a basis for an investment decision needs **SEBI Research Analyst (RA) registration**. This is the single most important unlock, and it's cheap: a graduate degree in any discipline, the NISM Series XV exam (₹1,500), ₹15,000 + GST registration, and a fixed-deposit lien.
6. **Regulation is your moat, not your constraint.** Only **6.3%** of prominent Indian finfluencers are SEBI-registered (CFA Institute, Jul 2026). Unregistered creators can't legally make stock calls. Brokers can't pay them, and educators who want broker money must use price data at least 30 days old. SEBI's AI system "Sudarshan" has already taken down 1.2 lakh posts. A **registered** research newsroom can do everything they can't.
7. **"Legal but edgy" works on attention, speed, pricing and distribution. It doesn't work on the securities themselves.** In securities law, most "grey" tactics (trading ahead of your own calls, undisclosed stock promotion, cherry-picked track records) aren't legal-but-unethical. They are violations that nobody has charged *yet*, and SEBI now finds them with AI. The Avadhut Sathe order (Dec 2025: ~₹546 cr impounded, ~₹601 cr disgorgement directed) shows the downside.
8. **The durable business isn't ads.** It's **paid research subscriptions (as an RA) + B2B research licensing + tools (PTIS)**. YouTube and Reels are distribution, and ads are a bonus.

## 1. Three findings that change the plan

### 1a. YouTube no longer pays for "AI persona gives finance advice"

- In July 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to **"inauthentic content"**. It enforced it in **January 2026**, removing channels with a combined ~35M subscribers from the Partner Program in one sweep.
- On **16 Jul 2026** it clarified three categories that can't be monetized:
  1. generic, template-based or mass-produced content;
  2. off-putting or distressing content;
  3. **AI personas discussing sensitive topics: finance, health, legal.** YouTube's examples were an AI doctor, **an AI podcast host giving investment advice**, and an AI persona interpreting laws.
- YouTube also said what is still allowed. A **real professional using AI in production** (scripts, charts, subtitles, even presentation) remains monetizable. The line is a *synthetic personality presented as a trustworthy human expert*.
- The "altered or synthetic content" disclosure toggle is required for realistic faces, voice cloning and generated scenes.

**Implication:** a made-up photoreal anchor ("Rohan, market analyst", who doesn't exist) that delivers stock views sits exactly in category 3. A real founder, named and accountable, whose delivery is sometimes an AI twin, does not. Design every format so that **a real person owns the analysis on screen**.

### 1b. India requires visible labels on realistic synthetic video

- **IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules 2026**, notified 10 Feb 2026 and in force from 20 Feb 2026, cover "synthetically generated information": audio or video that *realistically appears to be a real person or event*.
- Large platforms (over 50 lakh users: YouTube, Instagram, X) must make the uploader **declare** synthetic content, **verify** the declaration, and show a **continuous visible label** for the whole video, plus provenance metadata.
- AI-written *text* is outside the definition. AI scripts are fine without a label; AI faces and voices are not.

**Implication:** plan the brand assuming that **"AI-generated" will be stamped on every avatar video**. Make it part of the brand ("research by humans, delivered by our AI anchor"). Hiding it isn't possible, and getting caught hiding it is fatal to trust.

### 1c. The finance-on-social environment is hostile to anonymous realistic faces

- Deepfakes of the BSE CEO (warning issued Mar 2026) and the NSE chief have been used to push "stock tip" WhatsApp groups. A Bengaluru retiree lost over ₹61 lakh to a deepfake video in Jul 2026.
- SEBI's November 2025 warning says fraudsters *"pose as SEBI-registered intermediaries, prominent public figures…"*
- SEBI is working with Google and Meta on **advertiser verification for financial advertisers**, and runs AI-based takedowns ("Sudarshan"; 1.2 lakh posts removed by Mar 2026).

**Implication:** to Indian retail investors in 2026, *a realistic AI face talking about stocks* **looks like the scam**. A named person with a visible SEBI registration number on screen looks like the solution. That contrast is worth more than any visual polish.

## 2. The "realistic face" decision

| Option | Monetization risk | Trust | Cost & scale | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Fictional photoreal AI anchor** (a person who doesn't exist) | **High.** This is YouTube's category 3 for finance content | Low. Looks like the deepfake-scam pattern | Cheapest to scale | ❌ Don't use it for the flagship brand |
| **B. Founder's own digital twin** (HeyGen Avatar IV or similar, trained on your own face and voice, disclosed) | Low. A real, accountable expert using AI for delivery | Medium to high once the founder also appears live | $29–$149/month for tools. Scales daily formats | ✅ **Primary** for daily and short formats |
| **C. Stylized mascot or "research bot" character** (clearly non-human, like an animated analyst bot) | Low. Obviously AI, and it doesn't pretend to be a human expert | Medium. Signals "we're a tech company" | Cheap | ✅ Good for Reels, the AI-demo franchise, and the "our system flagged this" framing |
| **D. Voice-over + data visuals** (Hidden Economy style, no face) | Low if the narration is a real person or the disclosed twin, and the research is original | High for long-form | Visuals take the most effort | ✅ **Primary** for 15–25 min investigations |
| **E. Real founder on camera** | None | Highest | Doesn't scale | ✅ Required for about 1 in 5 videos, live streams, and the "were we right?" post-mortems |

**Recommended mix:** D for flagship investigations, B for daily pre-market and post-market reports, C for Reels and the AI experiments, and E regularly so the twin is anchored to a verifiable human.

Practical rules:
- Put the anchor's name and SEBI RA number (once you have it) in every lower-third.
- Keep the AI disclosure on screen for the whole video. Brand it rather than hide it.
- Don't use anyone else's face or voice (celebrities, CEOs, other creators), not even in satire. The 2026 rules have 2–3 hour takedown windows, and it's the exact pattern SEBI is hunting.
- Register the founder's likeness with YouTube likeness detection so that scammers who clone *your* twin get taken down. Once you're popular, they will try.

## 3. Legal architecture: go hard inside the lines

### 3a. What you can do at each license level

| Activity | Unregistered media / education | Media co. + **SEBI RA** (founder or entity) |
|---|---|---|
| Report events ("IRDAI released X at 6pm") | ✅ | ✅ |
| Explain mechanisms (commission → CAC → margin) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Broad market, index and macro commentary; sector trends; statistical summaries; historical base rates | ✅ These are explicitly excluded from "research report" | ✅ |
| **Company-specific view that gives a basis to invest** ("PB Fintech likely faces long-term bearish sentiment, don't expect a quick recovery") | ⚠️ This counts as research. Only safe if you quote a registered RA's report with their name, registration and disclosures (the journalist route) | ✅ With mandatory disclosures |
| Buy/sell, price targets, stop-losses | ❌ | ✅ |
| Get paid by brokers, AMCs or fintechs (sponsorship, affiliate, referral) | ❌ unless you're a pure educator using price data **≥30 days old** (uniform 30-day lag from 1 Jul 2026 under SEBI's circular of 8 May 2026) and making no recommendations or performance claims | ✅ Registered-to-registered association is allowed |
| Paid research subscription or premium channel | ❌ This is exactly what the Avadhut Sathe case was about | ✅ Fee cap ₹1,51,000 per family per year for individual/HUF clients |
| Sell "black-box" algo strategies to retail | ❌ | ✅ Needs RA registration **and** exchange empanelment as an algo provider (retail algo framework, mandatory for brokers from 1 Apr 2026) |
| Claim past performance | ❌ | ⚠️ Only in the prescribed way. SEBI's new PaRRVA (NSE + CARE) verifies return claims |

### 3b. The RA route (do this in Month 1)

- **Eligibility (individual):** a graduate degree in any discipline plus **NISM Series XV (Research Analyst)**. The exam fee is ₹1,500 and the certificate is valid for 3 years. The earlier experience requirement for non-finance graduates has been relaxed.
- **Registration:** ₹15,000 + GST for individuals. Applications go through SEBI, with RAASB (BSE) administering. A **fixed-deposit lien** that scales with client count replaced the old net-worth certificate; check the current RAASB schedule for amounts.
- **Obligations to design for from day one:**
  - **Trading restriction:** you and your associates can't trade a security you recommend or follow from **30 days before to 5 days after** a report. Every fresh update restarts the window. This kills front-running by design, and you can market that.
  - **AI disclosure:** RAs must disclose to clients how far AI tools are used. You're responsible for AI output and client-data security (2024 amendment; applied from 30 Apr 2025).
  - Mandatory disclosures on every public appearance and report: name, registration number, financial interest, conflicts.
  - Record-keeping for every research report and its rationale. Your PTIS pipeline can generate this audit trail automatically.
  - Keep clients segregated from any other business you run.
- Later, **convert to a corporate RA** when revenue justifies it, so the brand rather than one person holds the license.

### 3c. The alternative (or complementary) journalist route

SEBI's July 2025 RA FAQs say **journalists employed by media agencies don't need RA registration**. But any recommendation they make must be **based on reports by SEBI-registered analysts**, with name, registration status and financial-interest disclosures. So you can run the media company as a newsroom and **partner with an external RA** whose reports you cite. That works as a bridge while the founder's own registration is processed, but owning the license is better long-term.

### 3d. Data licensing

- Exchange price data is licensed. Redistributing NSE data, even 15-minute delayed data, as feeds or derived products needs a license through an **NSE-authorized vendor** (TrueData, Global Datafeeds and others) or NSE Data & Analytics.
- For videos, render charts from your licensed feed or from licensed embeds. Don't screen-grab broker terminals, and don't scrape and republish.
- Budget for a vendor license in the PTIS product plan. It is also a moat: casual creators won't pay for it.

### 3e. Platform hygiene

- YouTube: turn on the "altered or synthetic content" toggle on every avatar video. Avoid template sameness (vary structure, add original data every time).
- Instagram/Meta: AI label on synthetic media. Expect financial-advertiser verification if you run ads.
- Collect newsletter, WhatsApp and Telegram leads under the DPDP Act (explicit consent, purpose-limited).

## 4. The aggressive-but-legal playbook

The honest answer to "sometimes legal-but-not-ethical is normal business": in most industries there *is* a wide grey zone. **In Indian securities markets in 2026 the grey zone is narrow and now watched by AI.** The tactics that feel "edgy but legal" in finance content (trading around your own calls, paid stock shout-outs, showing only winning trades, a fake expert persona) are mostly **violations nobody has charged yet**, not legal moves. So aim the aggression where it's actually legal: **speed, attention, positioning, distribution, pricing, B2B**.

### Aggressive moves that are fully legal

1. **Research latency as the moat.** Regulators and companies often publish after 3:30 pm (IRDAI's paper came out on 23 Sep; the crash was on 24 Sep). Ship a **pre-market impact report by 9 pm**: an exposure map, the mechanism, historical analogues, and what to watch at the open. As an RA, that report can carry a view. Then run a **post-market "were we right?"** episode, which is a second video from the same research.
2. **Weaponize your registration.** 94% of big finfluencers can't do what you can. Market it bluntly: *"SEBI-registered research. Every call timestamped. We can't trade our own calls. Can yours say that?"* That is comparative positioning, not defamation, as long as it's true and you don't name individuals falsely.
3. **Public scoreboard.** Timestamp every view and publish a hit rate that includes the misses. Unregistered competitors legally *can't* make performance claims, so accountability becomes a feature only you can offer. Route formal return claims through PaRRVA once it covers you.
4. **Fill the broker vacuum.** Brokers and fintechs had to terminate contracts with unregistered finfluencers from January 2025, but they still need reach. A registered, AI-savvy research brand is the replacement partner. Pitch sponsored *education* series and co-branded investigations with clear disclosure.
5. **Newsjack every regulatory circular.** Be the fastest, clearest explainer for every SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA circular or consultation paper, plus budget and GST changes. These are high-intent searches with little competition in depth, and a pipeline can be first every time.
6. **Hooks that are provocative *and* true.** "PB Fintech's 36% crash isn't the real story." "The regulation that quietly killed online insurance lead-gen." Bold titles are allowed as long as the content substantiates them. Clickbait that under-delivers is the thing YouTube penalizes.
7. **One investigation, ten assets, four languages.** A flagship video becomes 3–5 Reels, a Shorts series, a carousel, a newsletter issue, an X thread and a pre-market card. **Update (26 Sep 2026): Hindi-first.** The primary language is Hindi with English financial terms, for reach and understanding (see [`hindi-script-guide.md`](hindi-script-guide.md)). Add an **English audio track** (and later Tamil/Telugu/Marathi, disclosed if AI-dubbed) to the same video. That recovers part of the English audience, where finance ad rates are roughly ₹80–₹250 per 1,000 views, 2–5× Hindi.
8. **Own the audience, not just the algorithm.** Run a WhatsApp Channel, Telegram broadcast and email newsletter, with a free daily brief and a paid RA tier. Platforms can demonetize you overnight; a list can't be taken away.
9. **Free tools as lead magnets.** An *"Event Impact Tracker"* page (regulatory events → exposed listed companies → historical reactions, using lagged or licensed data), or an *"AI analyst"* demo. Tools get shared; videos get forgotten.
10. **Show the AI failing.** "We gave AI 4 years of Nifty data. Here's the edge it found, and why it vanished after slippage." Honest failure content is rare, it goes viral, and it builds exactly the credibility that sells a paid research tier.
11. **Borrow authority.** Put registered RAs, ex-regulators, CFOs and insurance actuaries on camera for investigations. Their credibility transfers to your brand, and it's what YouTube wants to see ("real experts").
12. **Sell the research twice.** License the event-intelligence feed to brokers, PMS firms, small AMCs and regional media houses as a white-label pre-market desk. B2B revenue is steadier than creator revenue and doesn't depend on YouTube policy.

### Don't do these: they're illegal, not just "unethical"

| Tempting tactic | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Trade before publishing your own view, or sell into the pump | Front-running / fraud (PFUTP); also breaches the RA 30/5-day window |
| Paid promotion of a listed stock without disclosure | Fraud and manipulation risk; disclosure breach |
| Showing only winning trades or "student P&L" screenshots | This is exactly what the Avadhut Sathe order cited. SEBI found the trainers and participants were net losers |
| Telegram "premium calls" before you're registered | Unregistered RA/IA activity; money gets impounded |
| A fictional credentialed persona ("CA Priya Sharma, 15 yrs experience") | Misrepresentation; YouTube category 3; it's how scams present themselves |
| Scraping exchange data and redistributing it | Breaches exchange data licensing |
| Using a real CEO's or creator's face or voice, even as a joke | IT Rules 2026 takedowns in 2–3 hours; impersonation liability |
| Promising returns ("this system makes 3% a month") | Prohibited for everyone, registered or not |

## 5. The content system

### 5a. Franchises mapped to license level

| Franchise | Platform | Pre-RA (legal now) | Post-RA (adds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔎 **Market Investigations** | YouTube, 15–25 min | Mechanism, economics, sector exposure, history | Company-level view and scenarios |
| 🌙 **Pre-Market Desk** (evening event report) | YouTube short-form + WhatsApp/newsletter | "What changed, who is exposed, what history says" | "Our view for tomorrow's open" |
| ⚡ **60-Second Intelligence** | Reels/Shorts | Hooks on trending events, one surprising data point | Same, plus a link to the RA note |
| 🤖 **The Experiment** (AI + trading) | Reels + YouTube | Builds, backtests and failures on lagged data; teach the method | Live system demos, tool launches |
| 🧠 **Future Trader** | YouTube | Market structure, algo framework explainers, automation skills | Premium course or cohort (keep it strictly education) |
| 📊 **Were We Right?** | YouTube + community | — | Public scoreboard episodes |

### 5b. Pipeline with a built-in compliance gate

```
Sources (SEBI/RBI/IRDAI circulars, NSE/BSE filings, licensed prices, news)
  → Event detection (LLM classifier + rules)
  → Exposure map (entity graph: regulator → sector → listed companies → revenue lines)
  → Mechanism & earnings scenarios (LLM draft + analyst model)
  → Historical analogues (PTIS event study: T+1, T+5, T+20, T+60 reactions)
  → Draft script
  → COMPLIANCE GATE
      - classifier flags recommendation language / company-specific opinions
      - if flagged → RA review + disclosure block + trading-window log
      - AI-use disclosure appended
  → Human edit (real analyst owns the final say)
  → Production (twin / VO / mascot + charts from licensed data)
  → Distribution (YouTube, Reels, WhatsApp, newsletter)
  → Post-mortem + scoreboard update (feeds back into the analogue database)
```

The compliance gate isn't overhead. The records it produces are exactly what SEBI requires RAs to keep, and the post-mortems build the proprietary event database that becomes the PTIS moat.

### 5c. Worked example: IRDAI, 23–25 Sep 2026

- **23 Sep, evening:** the consultation paper comes out. It proposes that life insurers cut expenses of management (EoM) to 15% of gross direct premium within 2 years and 12.5% within 5; that general insurers move the EoM base to domestic GDPI with the limit going from 30% to 20% over 5 years; that commission limits vary by segment, channel and product complexity; plus conduct and transparency rules.
- **Pipeline output by 9 pm:** distributors and aggregators (PB Fintech, Turtlemint) have **high** exposure. Bancassurance-heavy lenders have medium to high exposure. Insurers' exposure is mixed: lower costs could offset lower growth. The mechanism is lower take-rate per policy, plus harder lead-gen, so volume can't easily compensate. The status is a *consultation, not final*: there is a glide path, and the final rules may soften.
- **24 Sep:** PB Fintech falls up to 36% to a ₹1,210 52-week low. Bajaj Finance and L&T Finance are also hit.
- **25 Sep:** PB Fintech falls another ~5% to ₹1,140. HSBC downgrades to Hold, Jefferies keeps Buy, and management estimates a 60–70% cut to health-business NPV.
- **Content produced from one event:** the pre-market desk report, the flagship investigation, 3 Reels, "were we right?", a 30-day follow-up and a final-regulation update. That's seven assets.
- **Legal difference:** *without* RA registration, the 23 Sep report can only map the exposure and the history. *With* RA registration, it can say "we expect sustained pressure on distributor valuations; here's what would change our view." That sentence is the premium product.

## 8. 90-day roadmap

**Weeks 1–4: Foundation**
- Register the Pvt Ltd company. Book NISM Series XV. File the RA application as soon as you pass.
- Lock the brand: real founder plus disclosed twin plus a mascot, and "Research by humans. Delivered with AI."
- Build pipeline v0: circular and filing watcher → event classifier → exposure-map template → script.
- Publish **pre-RA-safe content only**: regulatory explainers, historical base rates, sector mechanism deep dives, and AI experiments on lagged data.
- Start the WhatsApp Channel and newsletter from day 1.

**Weeks 5–8: Franchise testing**
- 2 flagship investigations, 3 pre-market desk episodes a week, 5 Reels a week, and 1 experiment.
- Track retention at 30 seconds and 50%, return-viewer %, newsletter sign-up rate per video, and share rate on Reels.
- Pitch 3 brokers or fintechs for sponsored education (as a registered-entity partnership once your RA is approved).

**Weeks 9–12: License goes live**
- Once the RA registration is live, add views to the pre-market desk. Launch the public scoreboard and a paid beta (100 subscribers is the goal).
- Publish the first "were we right?" episode.
- Build the first B2B demo (a white-label pre-market desk for one broker or media house).

**Scale / kill signals at day 90**
- Scale if the flagship's average view duration is at least 40%, there are 1,000+ newsletter sign-ups, 50+ paid beta subscribers, and at least one B2B conversation reaches pilot.
- Pivot the format (not the thesis) if Reels grow but YouTube long-form doesn't. Invert the funnel to Reels → newsletter → paid tier.

## 9. Risks to watch

- **Platform policy drift.** YouTube could tighten AI-persona rules further. Mitigate with the real-human anchor and your own lists.
- **A regulator may treat "event impact maps" as research** even without explicit calls. The RA license neutralizes this.
- **Being wrong in public.** The scoreboard makes misses visible. Treat that as the price of credibility, and design the post-mortem format to be interesting when you're wrong.
- **Key-person risk.** The license and face are tied to one founder. Plan the corporate RA and a second on-camera analyst by month 9–12.
- **Clone scams of your own twin.** Register likeness detection. Publish "we never DM tips" prominently.
