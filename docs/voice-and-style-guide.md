# Finmedia — Voice & Style Guide

*Calm, catchy, convincing, and built on deeper evidence than anyone else. Modeled on the style of "The Hidden Economy" (@TheHiddenEconomyTV) and designed to beat it on research. Research date: 26 Sep 2026.*

---

## 1. The reference: "The Hidden Economy"

**Which channel:** [**The Hidden Economy (@TheHiddenEconomyTV)**](https://www.youtube.com/@TheHiddenEconomyTV/videos). Its official landing page describes it as *"precious metals & commodities with John AG."* The channel decodes the world of precious metals with a deep focus on **gold and silver**, for a mostly US audience. (Several other channels share the name, including one hosted by "Richard Coleman"; this guide is about the John AG channel.)

The analysis below uses video titles found through search, plus the channel's own description. **Update:** a full transcript (the Fed-hike video) has been annotated line by line in [`transcript-teardown-100k-playbook.md`](transcript-teardown-100k-playbook.md). The team reports the channel averages ~6–8k views per video; that doc explains why and how we design for 100k+.

### 1a. What the channel actually publishes

**Titles found under the channel handle** (confirmed through search):
- *97.9% Will Fail With Gold & Silver in the Next Crisis — Do You Know?*
- *95% Don't Know About These Powerful Metals (5% Know The Secret)*
- *Silver IS SETTING A TRAP — Most Investors Won't See It Forming*
- *99% Will Lose With Gold & Silver — Are You Ready?*
- *Stop Buying the Silver Dip: Here's What the Mainstream Is Hiding From You | John Ag* (May 2026)

**Topics that appear in the same search results but that we couldn't confirm are from this channel:** central-bank gold buying ("What Do Central Banks Know That You Don't?"), silver market stress, gold/silver crash explainers, and underground/black-market economies.

**Observations:**
- **One niche, covered deeply and often.** Gold and silver, again and again, from many angles: crisis, central banks, "traps," dips. The audience comes back because the channel is *the* place for that one topic.
- **The presenter is a persona.** "Ag" is the chemical symbol for silver, so "John AG" is very likely a brand character, not a legal name. You've noted the face looks AI-generated. If so, that's the exact pattern YouTube said in Jul 2026 it won't monetize for finance: *AI personas giving financial advice.* Channels like this carry a platform risk we deliberately avoid.
- **Calm delivery under alarming titles.** The titles create fear or FOMO; the calm narration makes the fear feel credible rather than hysterical. That contrast is the core of the style.

### 1b. The title formula, decoded

| Element | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **The "X% will fail" split** | "97.9% Will Fail…", "99% Will Lose…" | Splits the audience into losers and the few who know. Viewers click to join the winning minority |
| **The secret minority** | "(5% Know The Secret)" | Exclusivity; watching the video feels like earning insider status |
| **The trap** | "Silver IS SETTING A TRAP" | Loss aversion. Avoiding a loss motivates more than a promised gain |
| **The hidden-truth frame** | "What the Mainstream Is Hiding From You" | Positions the channel as the only honest source |
| **The direct question** | "Do You Know?", "Are You Ready?" | Makes it personal; implies the viewer probably isn't ready |
| **Fake precision** | "97.9%" | An oddly exact number feels researched, **but nobody can verify where it comes from** |

**Our edge in one sentence:** their percentages are invented; **ours come from SEBI.** Indian regulators have published exactly the kind of "X% lose" statistics this formula runs on, and they're real:
- *"91% Lost Money in F&O Last Year. We Traced Where the Money Went."* (SEBI FY25 study: over 91% lost)
- *"99% of the Winners Were Machines."* (SEBI FY26: 99% of prop and FPI profits came from algo entities)
- *"93% of Young Traders Lost. Here's What the Data Says About the Rest."* (SEBI FY22–FY24 study)
- *"62% of Investors Follow Finfluencers. Only 6% of Finfluencers Are Registered."* (SEBI survey; CFA Institute 2026)

That's the same psychological pull, with a verifiable source on screen.

### 1c. A niche lesson we should copy

Their success comes from **owning one asset class deeply**. Gold and silver matter even more in India, where households have always stored wealth in gold. A dedicated recurring franchise, **"The Gold Desk (India),"** is worth testing. Topics could include:
- gold/silver ETF and fund flows (AMFI data);
- RBI's gold reserves (RBI data);
- import duty changes (Budget);
- the rupee–gold relationship;
- how Indian gold investment options compare (physical, ETFs, funds, GIFT City, and RBI's tokenized-gold exploration).

Build the research the same way as every other format: official data first, historical base rates second. Verify every figure before publishing. None of this has been researched yet.

### What makes that style work

The feeling of *"he knows something deeper that I don't, and I need to keep listening"* comes from a small set of repeatable techniques:

| Technique | What it does to the viewer | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The anomaly cold open** | Opens a question the brain can't leave unanswered | "In 1992, one man moved the entire Indian stock market. Not with capital. With a loophole in how banks settled government bonds." |
| **2. Calm, low-energy delivery** | Calm reads as confidence. People who shout are selling; people who speak slowly seem to know | Slow pace, lower pitch, long pauses before key numbers |
| **3. Extreme specificity** | Exact dates, amounts and names signal real research | "At 6:10 pm on the 23rd, after the market had closed…" |
| **4. Reframing** | The viewer believes they're seeing the real story behind the obvious one | "This isn't a story about insurance. It's a story about who pays to acquire a customer." |
| **5. Systems and incentives, not villains** | Explaining *why* the system behaves this way feels like insider knowledge | "Follow the commission, and every product design decision starts to make sense." |
| **6. The historical echo** | Pattern recognition feels like wisdom | "We've seen this exact setup before. In 2013, and again in 2018." |
| **7. Open loops and paced reveals** | Keeps retention high; the payoff is saved | "But that's not the part that matters. That comes later." |
| **8. Second-person stakes** | Turns an abstract story into the viewer's own money | "If you own a SIP, you're already part of this story." |
| **9. The quiet ending** | A forward-looking close that makes the channel feel indispensable | "The regulation is still a proposal. What happens next is what we'll be watching." |

### Where that genre is weak, and where we win

This genre (gold-and-silver crisis media) often leans on **mystery and fear instead of evidence**: "the mainstream is hiding it," "the next crisis," invented percentages, few sources on screen. It *feels* deep, but viewers can't verify it. We'll use the same storytelling techniques, backed by stronger evidence:

| They (genre typical) | Us |
|---|---|
| Invented precision ("97.9% will fail") | **Real statistics from SEBI/RBI studies**, with the source on screen |
| Authority from mystery | **Authority from receipts.** The actual document is highlighted on screen |
| "Experts say…" | Named source, page number, date |
| Averages and dramatic single examples | **N, median and range** ("in 23 cases…") |
| Collapse and fear framing | Mechanism + scenarios + "what would prove us wrong" |
| Global/US-centric | **India-first depth** (SEBI, RBI, IRDAI, NSE data) plus global context |
| Narration only | **Our own data:** event studies, backtests, AI experiments |
| AI persona as "expert" (a monetization risk for finance under YouTube's Jul 2026 policy) | Real named analyst; any AI delivery is disclosed |
| Story ends at the insight | Story ends at **an action** (what to watch, what to learn, what to build) |

**Our positioning line:** *"Other channels tell you there's a hidden economy. We show you the documents."*

## 2. The house voice

### Personality
- **Calm, certain, a little understated.** Like someone who read the 140-page circular so you don't have to, and found the one paragraph that matters.
- **Never hypes. Never begs.** No "SMASH that like button," no "this will SHOCK you."
- **Respects the viewer's intelligence.** Explains mechanisms, doesn't define textbook terms unless the story needs it.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** "We don't know yet" said calmly is a power move.

### Delivery specs
- **Pace:** calm, about 10–15% slower than normal conversation for long-form (for the English track, ~130–145 words/min); a little faster for Reels.
- **Pauses:** a 0.5–1 s pause *before* every key number or reveal, marked `[beat]` in scripts.
- **Pitch:** low and steady. Energy rises only at the turn (section 3).
- **Sentences:** mostly short. One idea each. Occasionally one long sentence to build tension, then a short one to land it.
- **Music:** low ambient bed (drones, sparse piano, soft pulses). It drops out completely under the key reveal.

### Word choices

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Here's what most people missed." | "What they don't want you to know." |
| "The document says…" / "On page 42…" | "Sources say…" / "Experts believe…" |
| "In 23 comparable cases…" | "Always," "never," "guaranteed" |
| "This is still a proposal." | Treating proposals as done deals |
| "Here's what would change our view." | "100% sure," "trust me" |
| "The mechanism is simple." | Jargon walls |
| "Quietly," "notice," "look closer," "follow the money" | "Crash!!", "BOOM," "multibagger," "rocket," "jackpot" |

### Language strategy
- **Hindi-first (decided 26 Sep 2026):** Hindi with English financial terms, the way people actually talk about money in India. **English is a secondary audio track** on the same video. Full rules are in [`hindi-script-guide.md`](hindi-script-guide.md).
- Hindi keeps the same calm register. Don't slip into the loud "bhai log" register of trading Telegram.

## 3. Script structures

### 3a. Flagship documentary (15–25 min)

| Time | Section | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | **Cold open**: the anomaly | One specific, surprising fact + an open question. No intro, no logo |
| 0:45–1:30 | **The promise** | "By the end of this video you'll understand X, and why it matters for Y." |
| 1:30–5:00 | **Context**: how things worked before | The system, the incentives, the numbers |
| 5:00–9:00 | **The mechanism** | How it really works, the part nobody explains. Receipts on screen |
| 9:00–10:00 | **The turn** | "But that's not the real story." Reframe |
| 10:00–14:00 | **Evidence & history** | Case files, event study, N/median/range, the counter-case |
| 14:00–16:00 | **What it means for you** | Second-person stakes, scenarios, what would prove us wrong |
| 16:00–17:00 | **What to watch / what to do** | Concrete signals, a skill or system to build |
| 17:00–end | **Quiet close + open loop** | A forward-looking line + a tease for the next investigation |

Retention devices: an open loop every ~90 seconds; a receipt (document, chart) at least every 60 seconds; a chapter title card at each section change.

### 3b. Pre-Market Desk (6–10 min)
Cold open (the event, the timestamp) → what changed (the document) → who is exposed (map) → the mechanism → what history says (N, range) → proposal vs. final → what to watch at 9:15 am → disclosure card.

### 3c. Reel (35–60 s)
1. **0–2 s hook:** the single most surprising verified number, as on-screen text and voice.
2. **2–15 s:** the reframe ("That's not a coincidence. It's a transfer.").
3. **15–40 s:** the mechanism, in one visual.
4. **40–55 s:** the payoff (one edge, one action, or one thing to watch).
5. **Last 5 s:** "The full investigation is on our channel." Calm, no begging.

## 4. Titles and thumbnails

**Title formulas (curious, calm, specific):**
- *The ₹61,000 Crore Transfer Nobody Talks About*
- *Why India's Insurance Middlemen Just Lost Their Business Model*
- *RBI Already Moved ₹17,000 Crore on Tokenized Rails. Almost Nobody Noticed.*
- *The Loophole That Let One Man Move the Entire Market (1992)*
- *99% of the Winners Were Machines*
- *What Happened the Last 23 Times India Did This*

- *91% Lost Money in F&O Last Year. We Traced Where the Money Went.* (the "X%" split, with a real source)
- *The Trap Inside Expiry-Day Options: 59% of Trading, and Who's on the Other Side*

Rules: a specific number or date where possible; no ALL CAPS; no "SHOCKING"; the title must be literally true and substantiated in the video. **Every percentage in a title must come from a named official study, and that study must be shown on screen within the first 60 seconds.**

**Thumbnail language:** dark, cinematic background; one highlighted document excerpt or one number; at most 3–4 words; a muted palette with one accent color (our brand color) on the key element. When the real presenter appears, the expression is calm and focused, never a shocked face.

## 5. Visual language

- **"Receipts" motif:** the actual PDF page, with the key line highlighted and the source and date in the corner. This is our signature.
- **Timeline bars** with IST timestamps and market open/close markers.
- **Money-flow animations** (who pays whom), consistent across videos.
- **Charts** from licensed data, in the same style every time; N and range always visible on base-rate charts.
- **Archival material** for history episodes (newspaper front pages, parliamentary records), used within licensing and fair use.
- **AI disclosure** badge whenever synthetic media is used, styled as part of the brand ("Delivered by our AI anchor · Research by [Name], SEBI RA [number]").

## 7. Lines we don't cross (even for retention)

- **No conspiracy framing** ("elites," "they don't want you to know") without documented evidence. It erodes credibility, and YouTube's Jul 2026 policy de-monetizes content it finds "off-putting."
- **No fake urgency** ("buy before Monday").
- **No collapse porn.** Scenarios, not prophecies.
- **No implied insider-trading accusations.** Describe the observable timeline only.
- **No undisclosed synthetic media**, and never anyone else's face or voice.
- **No stock calls without RA registration and disclosures** (see the strategy doc).

## 8. Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Cold open has one specific, verified, surprising fact
- [ ] Every number has a citation in the research brief
- [ ] At least one on-screen "receipt" per minute
- [ ] Base rates show N + median + range
- [ ] Proposal vs. final status stated
- [ ] "What would change our view" included
- [ ] Ends with an action or a watch-list, plus an open loop
- [ ] No banned words; title literally true
- [ ] AI disclosure + human owner named
- [ ] Compliance tier checked (E / M / RA)
